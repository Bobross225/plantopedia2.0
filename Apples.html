Apples

  1. Pick the Right Variety
    Choose low-chill apples like Anna, Dorsett Golden, or Ein Shemer that thrive in Alabama's mild winters.
  2. Plant at the Right Time
    Set trees in late winter or early spring while dormant, on mild (45–70°F) days without hard freezes.
  3. Choose a Sunny Spot
    Apples need 6–8 hours of direct sunlight daily. Morning sun is best to keep leaves dry and healthy.
  4. Water Weekly
    Trees need about 1 inch of water per week. If rain is short, water deeply, especially during hot summers.
  5. Mulch & Prune
    Add 2–4 inches of mulch to keep roots cool and moist. Each late winter, prune to open the canopy for light and airflow.
  6. Protect & Harvest
    Cover trees during late frosts, and check for disease after wet weather. Harvest when apples twist off easily and taste sweet.
